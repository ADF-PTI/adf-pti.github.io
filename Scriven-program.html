Why solo running wasn't moving the needle

The commonest reason a beep score stalls: running everything at one steady, medium pace. That pace is a bit too hard to build a big easy base, and a bit too easy to grow your VO2 max — so you plateau, and it feels like effort with no reward. You're not doing anything wrong; the structure just wasn't there.

This block fixes it with two levers: easy running to grow the aerobic engine, and short, genuinely hard intervals — which is the single most effective thing for lifting a beep test — plus shuttle practice so the turning and pacing become second nature. Do those consistently for eight weeks and the score moves.

The three effort levels
Easy (Zone 2)

Conversational — full sentences without gasping. Feels almost too slow. This builds the engine; don't rush it.

Tempo (threshold)

"Comfortably hard" — controlled discomfort, only a few words at a time. Steady and even, never a sprint.

Hard (VO2)

RPE 8–9 — strong and fast, breathing hard, but repeatable. The last rep should feel tough but completeable, not an all-out sprint on rep one.

Beep-test technique & pacing
  • Turn on the line, not past it. Overrunning adds metres every shuttle and burns you out early — this is the biggest free win.
  • Don't lead the beep. Reach the line just as it sounds; sprinting early then waiting wastes energy.
  • Run efficient straight lines, plant and pivot, use your arms on the turn.
  • Settle in early and relax — the first levels should feel easy. Breathe rhythmically.
  • Warm up properly (the session warm-ups here are the template) and be hydrated.
Upper body — small gym is plenty

Dumbbells, bands and bodyweight cover everything needed for "general upper-body strength." The plan alternates a push-focused and pull-focused session and progresses by adding a little load or a rep or two when the last one felt solid.

Hand: keep grips relaxed and neutral early, and see the Notes tab for working around the brace.

Autoregulate

Green day and the session feels easy? Push the hard reps a touch. Flat or a poor sleep? Take the easy option or trim a set — a slightly easier session beats a skipped one. Consistency over eight weeks beats three heroic weeks and a flame-out.

Hand & load notes
Hand — working around the brace

Brace on for now, per your physio, and off when they clear it. Keep grips relaxed, favour neutral/hammer grips, and use fists or push-up handles if wrist/hand extension is sore. Progress upper-body load as the brace comes off — grip endurance returns quickly.

The post-session ache

Your physio's said the mild-to-moderate ache after PT is return-to-use, not the injury — so it's expected and fine to train through at that level.

Stop & check with physio if

Sharp (not dull) pain in the hand, swelling, or any loss of grip strength. That's different from the normal ache — back off and flag it.

Load management around JMET PT

These three sessions sit around your work PT. If JMET has a hard run the day before an interval day, keep that run controlled — don't arrive at the hard session already cooked. Two hard days in a row is the thing to avoid.

Readiness — what each light means
Green

Slept well, legs fresh, hand quiet. Full session — push the hard reps.

Amber

Flat or sore. Take the easy option, trim a set, or swap a hard day for an easy run.

Red

Wiped or hand flared. Easy movement or rest. Recovery is when the fitness actually lands.
