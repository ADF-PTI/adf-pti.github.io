The brief

What we're building

Heavy upper-body emphasis, the rest of the body maintained, and everything worked around a shin-splint history that's still settling. Two-thirds of the weekly volume is upper body; the pull-up and the push-up are the headline jobs.

  • 1First strict pull-up, then reps — from a start of not reaching halfway.
  • 2Push-ups at full depth with form held across the whole set — no fade, no shallow reps.
  • 3Full-body strength maintained, upper body driven hard.
  • 4A durable base for RMC, built without aggravating the shins.

The shins set the rules

No jumping, no plyometrics, no high-impact work anywhere in this program. Running stays with your separate return-to-run program; any extra conditioning here is bike, row or incline walk only. This complements your return-to-run and physio guidance — it doesn't replace them. Pain at rest, or symptoms getting worse: back off impact and review.

Why the two movements aren't working yet

Pull-up — stuck below halfway

Not a strength ceiling — a missing bottom end. You don't yet own the scapular pull and the dead-hang-to-mid range. We build it from the hang upward, one rung at a time, and you should move up a rung every fortnight.

Push-up — depth and fade

Two separate problems. Shallow depth is strength and range, fixed with tempo and pause-at-the-bottom reps. Form falling apart late in a set is trunk endurance — the hips sag when the core tires — so we train anti-extension core hard.

The centrepiece

Pull-up ladder

Work at your current rung. Re-test every two weeks and move up as soon as you can. Slot the rung you're on into Session B, and touch it lightly in Session D.

1

Dead hang

Accumulate 60s total across sets. Builds grip and shoulder tolerance.

2

Scapular pull-ups

Hang, pull the shoulder blades down and back without bending the arms. This is the piece you're missing.

3

Inverted rows

Horizontal pulling base. Lower the bar to make it harder as you get stronger.

4

Band-assisted pull-ups

Full range every rep. Thick band early, thinner band over the weeks.

5

Negative (eccentric) pull-ups

Step or jump to the top, lower under control for 5 seconds. Your biggest strength builder — prioritise it in Phase 2.

6

Top isometric holds

Chin over the bar, hold 5–10s. Owns the hardest position.

7

First strict rep — then build

One clean rep, then add reps week on week.

Across the block

Progression & re-test

Weeks 1–4 · Build the base

Groove technique, bank volume

Push-ups: tempo and full range come before rep count. Pull-ups: scap work plus band-assist, start bringing in negatives. Effort around RPE 7–8. Each week, add a rep or shave the band assistance slightly. End of Week 4: re-test, then a light deload (sets cut ~40%) if you're run down, or roll straight into Phase 2.

Weeks 5–8 · Turn up intensity

Load and hold the hard positions

Push-ups: floor tempo, then deficit push-ups (hands on plates for extra depth), add a little load if strong. Pull-ups: negatives at 5s, then top holds, then attempt your first assisted-to-unassisted rep; drop to a thinner band. Lat pulldown heavier. Effort around RPE 8.

Re-test — end of Week 4 and Week 8

  • Max strict push-ups to a set form standard (full depth, straight body, no sag). Record the count.
  • Pull-up milestone — dead-hang time plus your current ladder rung.
  • Plank max hold — trunk-endurance marker; it should climb as push-up form holds longer.

Running it in

How to run it

  • Four days a week. Keep the heavy upper days (A and B) off the back of heavy scheduled PT where you can.
  • Protect the shins around running. Don't stack Session C or any impact next to a run day — flank run days with upper sessions instead.
  • Warm up shoulders and wrists every upper day. Non-negotiable given the pressing volume.
  • Grease the groove (optional, high value). On off-days, scatter a few easy sub-max sets of push-ups and scap or negative pull-ups through the day. Frequent easy practice builds skill-strength fast.
  • Only three days some weeks? Run A, B and C, and fold Session D's key sets in as a finisher on A and B. Keep the fortnightly re-test.

Complements — does not replace — the return-to-run program and physio guidance. No jumping, plyometrics or high-impact until cleared. Quality over numbers, especially in Phase 1: full range, controlled tempo.
